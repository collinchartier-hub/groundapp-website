Accessibility

groundapp.live · Last updated: November 10, 2026

We want everyone to be able to use groundapp.live, whether you use a keyboard, a screen reader, captions, zoom or reduced motion. This page says what standard we aim for, what we checked, what still falls short, and how to reach us.

The standard

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 at level AA. That is the standard Norway's rules on universal design ask of websites.

This statement covers the website groundapp.live. It does not cover the GroundApp app for iPhone and Apple Watch, or the App Store pages we link to.

What we checked, and when

On November 10, 2026 we checked every public page: the home page, the blog and its five posts, the three Breath Atlas practices, Bodie's Breath Run, the masterclass waiting room, support, privacy, terms, delete account, the legal notice, this page and the page you see when a link is broken.

  • Automated test: axe-core 4.14 with the WCAG 2.0 A and AA rules, on a phone width (390 px) and a desktop width (1440 px). Every page now passes with no errors.
  • By hand: using the whole site with only a keyboard, visible focus, skip links, text for images, captions and transcripts, colour contrast, page language, headings, form labels and error messages, link text, zooming to 200%, controls for motion and sound, and page titles.

What we fixed

  • Collin's intro film now has captions all the way through and a written transcript.
  • The three Breath Atlas practices now have a written transcript next to the audio.
  • A Pause motion button stops Bodie's breathing and the app recordings on the home page. The breathing circle on the broken link page and the scenery in Bodie's Breath Run can be paused too.
  • Email forms show their labels above the fields, not only inside them.
  • The swipe rows of app screens on phones can be scrolled with a keyboard.
  • On the smallest phone screens (320 px wide) the top bar fits without overlapping, and the game menu keeps its text readable over the scene.

What still falls short

  • Bodie's Breath Run: the game is played with one button (Space, the mouse or a touch), so it works with a keyboard. The game itself is drawn in 3D, so a screen reader can read its menus and buttons but not what happens during a run. Some modes are about timing and holding your breath, so they can't give unlimited time.
  • Bodie on the home page: he is a 3D drawing. A screen reader hears who he is, and Space or Enter lets you breathe with him. The live breath count under him is not read out; the technique you picked is.
  • The intro film: where Collin talks to the camera, the captions are part of the picture, so you can't resize or turn them off there. The transcript has every word.
  • Screenshots of the app contain text from the app. Each one has a written description, and the page text says the same thing.
  • Breath Atlas: the audio and its transcript open after you enter an email address.

Tell us about a problem

If something on the site doesn't work for you, email collin@groundapp.live. Tell us the page and what happened, and we'll fix it or find you another way to get the same thing. We reply within 2 business days.

If you are in Norway and you're not happy with our answer, you can contact Uutilsynet, the Norwegian authority for universal design of ICT, at uutilsynet.no.
