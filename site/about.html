About GroundApp

From the mind into the body, this is where we feel grounded.

Breathwork that meets you where you are

GroundApp exists for one purpose: to help you regulate your nervous system with the most direct tool every human being has. Your breath.

Most people spend their days stuck in stress mode. Tight shoulders, shallow breathing, a mind that will not quiet down. That's your sympathetic nervous system running the show. GroundApp gives you the guidance and the structure to shift out of fight-or-flight and into calm, clarity and presence whenever you need it.

Built by a coach, not a tech company

Collin Chartier is a breathwork and mental performance coach and a longtime endurance athlete. Breathwork started for him in endurance sport, where he learned that controlling his breath was the most useful tool he had for managing stress, focus and performance under pressure.

After coaching breathwork with athletes, professionals and people dealing with anxiety and burnout, Collin kept seeing the same pattern. People wanted to breathe better, but they didn't know which technique to use, when to use it, or how to build a practice that sticks. Most apps handed them a timer and a menu and left the rest up to them.

GroundApp is Collin's answer. Not a tech product looking for a wellness angle, but a coach's tool. He's the only instructor in the app, and the guided sessions are narrated in his voice. (To be straight about how: the narration is an AI model of Collin's voice, trained on his own recordings.)

Why breathwork works

Breathing is the one part of your autonomic nervous system you can also run on purpose. Change how you breathe and your heart rate changes with it, often within a few breaths. That makes your breath the most direct lever you have on how you feel.

Your autonomic nervous system has two branches. The sympathetic branch drives "fight or flight": faster heart rate, shallow breathing, heightened alertness. The parasympathetic branch is "rest and digest": calm, recovery and repair. Constant stress, screens and overstimulation keep a lot of us stuck on the sympathetic side. Breathwork gives you a way to shift.

The Long Exhale

Your heart rate rises a little as you breathe in and slows as you breathe out. That slowing comes from the vagus nerve. Long, slow exhales give it more time to work, which is why calming techniques lean on the exhale.

CO2 Tolerance

Your urge to breathe is driven mostly by carbon dioxide, not a lack of oxygen. Slow breathing and calm breath holds build your tolerance to it. GroundApp's free BOLT test gives you a simple way to track yours.

Heart Rate Variability

Breathing at around five to six breaths a minute, often called coherent or resonance breathing, produces big swings in heart rate variability. HRV is one of the clearest windows we have into how your nervous system is coping.

A Trainable Skill

Shifting your state on purpose gets easier with reps. A few minutes most days beats one long session a month. Streaks, a heatmap and your mood trend in GroundApp show the reps adding up.
