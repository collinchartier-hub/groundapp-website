Different breathing tools serve different people. A simple timer can be useful. So can a recorded guide or a session with a person. I am not interested in dismissing one to make another sound better.

## What I built

GroundApp starts with a check-in: name how you feel, rate its intensity and your energy, then see a suggested session. You can also choose a technique and read its guide.

Local matching uses app rules. Optional Bodie AI plans are a separate feature with a separate data flow, described in the [privacy policy](/privacy). Neither measures a person's nervous system or knows what care they need.

The guided narration uses an AI model of my voice, trained on my recordings. I want that clear before someone listens.

GroundApp runs on iPhone and Apple Watch. With permission, Apple Health provides a separate health-data connection. Compatible Bluetooth monitors and devices broadcasting heart rate are another connection.

## What I hope future tools will do

This section is opinion, not a list of shipped features.

I would like tools to explain suggestions in plain language, make it easier to say "not today," and help someone notice which practices they choose to return to.

A future tool might use more context, but that would need a clear purpose, appropriate consent and a way to opt out. More data is not automatically better guidance.

## What I would not hand to an app

A conversation with a coach is not the same as a generated plan. A licensed professional can work within a clinical relationship an app does not provide. Software should not imply that it replaces either.

It should be easy to stop, easy to understand the data flow and possible to make a choice without being pushed by a streak or a score. Those are design values, not a claim that every future feature is already solved.

## Privacy is part of the product

Check-in answers and health readings are personal. The policy explains app data, optional AI processing, website marketing and retention separately. It is worth reading those details before turning a feature on.

I would rather describe the current behavior plainly than promise a future capability.
